Support

Turning it on, using it, and what to do when a capture looks wrong.

Turn it on

On a Mac

  1. Open the Whole Page Screenshot app once. Its window shows whether the extension is on.
  2. Click Quit and Open Safari Settings…, or in Safari choose Safari › Settings › Extensions.
  3. Tick Whole Page Screenshot. No website access is needed: Safari lets it capture a page when you click its button.

Then open any web page, such as the demo page, and click the Whole Page Screenshot button in the toolbar. If you don’t see the button, choose View › Customize Toolbar and drag it in.

On iPhone and iPad

  1. Open the Whole Page Screenshot app once.
  2. Open Settings, then Apps, then Safari, then Extensions, and tap Whole Page Screenshot.
  3. Turn on Allow Extension. No website access is needed.

Then, on any web page, tap the page menu button beside the address, then Whole Page Screenshot.

Use it

  • The whole page: click Whole Page in the popup, or press ⌃⇧S. Keep the page in front while it scrolls; the editor opens when it’s done.
  • Just the screen: click Visible Area.
  • Mark it up: in the editor, draw arrows, rectangles, ellipses, text and highlights; blur or pixelate anything private; crop. ⌘Z undoes.
  • Take it with you: Copy puts the picture on the clipboard. Save writes PNG, JPEG or PDF to your Downloads folder.
  • Right-click: choose Capture Whole Page or Capture Visible Area on any page.
  • On iPhone and iPad: pinch to zoom the picture, and double-tap a text to edit it. Save prepares the file, then Share opens the share sheet: Save to Files, Save Image, Mail, Messages or AirDrop.

Common questions

The header appears in the middle of the picture

Whole Page Screenshot shows fixed headers and sticky bars once, where they belong. A few sites build their header in a way it can’t tell apart from content. In Options, try Fixed headers: On every screen to see the page exactly as it scrolls.

Pictures are missing, or the bottom is cut off

Some sites load content slowly as you scroll. In Options, set Slow pages to Wait a little or Wait longer, and capture again. Pages that keep growing (endless feeds) stop at Longest capture; raise it to get more.

The picture is darker than the page

An extension that darkens websites, such as a dark mode extension, changes what Safari shows, and the screenshot shows what Safari shows. Turn that extension off for the site, or switch to light appearance, and capture again.

It says it can’t capture this page

Safari doesn’t let extensions capture its own pages, such as Settings, the Start Page or other extensions, or App Store pages. Try a normal web page.

The capture stopped when I switched tabs

Safari can only take a screenshot of the tab in front, so the page needs to stay in front until the capture finishes. Capture it again and wait for the editor to open.

The picture is too large to save

Very long pages are saved in parts, numbered in order. If a part still can’t be saved, choose Resolution: Standard (1×) in Options, or crop the picture first.
